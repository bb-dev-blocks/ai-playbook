## Draft citation verdicts

- **Takshashila DD 2026-23 (Madhavan & Shinde, 22 Jul 2026)**: REAL. The title, authors, date and number all match (R01). Every draft claim appears in the text, but most are secondary (the paper cites press). Details:
  - "$2B over 24 months": the paper says "India is reportedly going to spend as much as $2 billion on purchasing military drones over the next 24 months" (Introduction, PDF p.3). The underlying source is Reuters, 3 Jun 2026 (R07): Drone Federation India president Smit Shah said "tactical drone procurements in India may exceed ₹20,000 crore, or more than $2 billion", with deliveries over 18–24 months. This is an industry estimate, not a government figure. Cite it as "reported".
  - "Rs 40,000 Cr EP post-Sindoor": the paper says "a substantial share of the ₹40,000 crore sanctioned for Emergency Procurement (EP)… earmarked for… surveillance drones, kamikaze drones and loitering munitions" (fn 15 cites TOI, 17 May 2025). Independently confirmed by TOI and ET/ANI (R16): EP-6 has an "overall outer limit of around Rs 40,000 crore", contracts capped at Rs 300 Cr each, finalised within 40 days, deliveries within 1 year. The drone share is NOT quantified anywhere found.
  - "6–8 week EW adaptation": the paper says "the enemy's EW units take only six to eight weeks to adapt their jammers" (fn 11, citing CSIS). The fetched CSIS text (R17) does not contain "six to eight weeks". A secondary blog attributes "approximately six weeks" to RUSI via CSIS. Treat as weakly sourced.
  - Ashni platoons: the paper says "all 385 infantry battalions, equipped with ten drones—four for surveillance and six for loitering munitions". The number differs from the Army's: ET/PTI (R15) quotes "each of the 380 infantry battalions now has an Ashney… platoon… at least four surveillance drones and six are of armed category". Use **380** and cite R15.
  - Bhairav: the paper says "A total of 25 battalions, with approximately 250 personnel each". SUPPORTED by ET (R15): "we will have 25 such battalions in the next six months". The Tribune (R24) says 5 were operationalised with a plan for 25. (Earlier ET, Aug 2025, said "40 to 50" in phases, so the target has varied.)
  - Divyastra: the paper says "five regiments are planned by reorganising existing gun batteries". CNBC-TV18 (R23): "in the first phase, at least five regiments of the artillery will have a 'Divyastra' battery each". ET Jan 2026 (R22) says "around 35-40 Divyastra batteries". The numbers have since grown.
  - Shaktiban: the paper says "Three such regiments are planned". This conflicts with ET Jan 2026 (R22): "raising 15-20 Shaktibaan regiments".
  - Rudra: the paper says "Three such brigades have already been operationally validated". The press I found (ET Aug 2025, Tribune) says "two Rudra Brigades have already been raised". I couldn't confirm three.
  - "1 lakh personnel / 30,000 drones in 12 months": the paper says "target of training one lakh personnel in drone operations, employing 30,000 drones within a year" (no footnote). "Over a lakh drone operatives" pool: ET (R22). The 30,000-drone figure was NOT independently found. Newer data: the Army says ~50,000 trained in the past year with a 5-lakh-in-5-years roadmap (R28), and a drone fleet "beyond 50,000" (COAS, R27).
  - SSU contract model: SUPPORTED. This is the paper's own recommendation: "Supply, Service, and Upgrade (SSU) contracts… similar to the LTSA the DPM suggests, but would not be restricted to foreign OEMs" (Recommendations).
- **Draft DAP 2026** (R05): REAL. Released 10 Feb 2026 (R21), not mid/late 2025 as the draft assumed. Verified:
  - OCPP stands for **"Other Capital Procurement Procedure"**, not "Optimum Cost…". It is Chapter VIII (ToC PDF p.3, chapter ~PDF p.97). Its scope is "ROH / OH, refits, upgrades, refurbishment, modification and replacement of unserviceable units".
  - "The quantities included will have inherent 25% flexibility in numbers or cost" (Ch VIII para 3).
  - "timelines for OCPP procurements will be reduced by four weeks" (para 8).
  - "allows the Services to procure commercial-off-the-shelf (COTS) drone swarms… with minimal desirable customisation" (preface, PDF p.4).
  - "engines for RPVs / Drones manufactured in India and semiconductors… manufactured in Fabs in India shall be taken up as projects of National importance and will mandatorily be procured… These procurements will not be considered as Single Vendor Cases" (PDF p.29). This is directly relevant to an indigenous chip pitch.
- **DPM 2025** (R06): REAL. The draft's "15% upgrade buffer" EXISTS BUT THE CLAIM DIFFERS. It is a "Growth of Work" provision for repair and refit, not a drone upgrade buffer. The text reads: "Additional financial sanction up to 15% of contract value (including taxes and duties) shall be catered for such GoW at AoN stage" (ship repair/refit, printed p.(196)-area, PDF p.279). There is a parallel GoW clause for "Aviation Repair & Overhaul (ROH) Contracts". PIB (R20): "upfront provision for 15% Growth of work in case of Repair/Refits of ships & Repair/Overhauling of Aviation equipment". DPM Vol I contains no "drone/RPV/UAV" text at all; drone applicability is Takshashila's interpretation. Other verified provisions:
  - LTSA = "**Long Term Support Agreement**", listed as a Sustenance Contract type for equipment "procured without ToT… in small quantity" (para 9.3.6, PDF p.210).
  - Option clause allows "additional quantity up to a maximum of 50% of the originally contracted quantity" (para 6.35.1).
  - Separately, ICT mid-course corrections may be "not exceeding 15% of the original contract value" as Paid Change Requests.
- **"Lt Col Nikhil Srivastava et al."**: the author is REAL, but the citation is wrong. It is a single-author web article (no "et al."), "Navigating Small Drones in an Electronic Warfare Environment", dras.in, 22 Nov 2025 (R08). He is a retired Artillery officer (1993–2015) and the article is not peer-reviewed.
  - Supports: GNSS jamming/spoofing threat; "portable jammers of 10–40 watts can create GNSS blackout zones several kilometres wide"; VIO "is now a primary navigation method for small drones operating without GNSS"; "low-latency visual–inertial pipelines operating below 30 milliseconds".
  - Does NOT cover PUF or DPA.
  - No Google Scholar/IEEE/arXiv paper by this author found.
- **"Ashu Nayak et al."**: the author is REAL, but it is a single-author paper (no "et al."): R09, ECC Summit 2024, DOI 10.17051/ECC/02.02.08.
  - Supports: PUF + secure boot + crypto coprocessor; resistance to "side-channel attacks (… DPA and timing analysis)"; "latency and power overhead… (<10%)"; a secure drone flight controller case study on an FPGA (firmware injection, GPS spoofing mentioned as threats).
  - Does NOT cover VIO.
  - Venue is low-profile. Weak for investor credibility.
- **ideaForge DRHP/AR — ">400,000 flights"**: exists-but-claims-differ. No source says 400,000. The figure grows by source:
  - DRHP (Feb 2023): ">300,000 flights as of Dec 31 2022" (per Hensex IPO note).
  - RHP (Jun 2023): "over 350,000 flights… as of March 31, 2023" (HDFC IPO note).
  - AR FY24 (R10): "Our customers have completed over 450,000 flights using our UAVs" as on 31 Mar 2024, and "on average, an ideaForge drone takes off every five (5) minutes".
  - Website (R26, undated): "over 1 Million flights".
  - Use 450,000 (AR FY24, dated) or 1M (website, undated).
- **Garuda Aerospace "500,000 agri drones"**: NOT FOUND. The CEO Agnishwar Jayaprakash's actual statements:
  - Jun 2024 (R18): "We plan to sell 25,000 agri-drones in the next two years and at least 25,000 other consumer drones".
  - Jun 2025 (PIB, R19): "vision… to manufacture one lakh Made-in-India drones by 2026. We have delivered 4,000 drones so far".
  - No 2024-25 annual report found (private company).
  - The claim is likely off by 10× (50,000) or a mix-up with 1 lakh. Drop it or correct it.
- **Drone Rules 2021** (R02): REAL, G.S.R. 589(E), 25 Aug 2021.
- **Drone (Amendment) Rules 2022** (R04): REAL, G.S.R. 108(E), 11 Feb 2022. It replaced "Remote Pilot Licence" with "Remote Pilot Certificate" issued by an authorised RPTO. It did not remove all pilot credentialing; per officials, no RPC is needed for non-commercial drones ≤2 kg (ET/TOI).
- **QCI CSUAS** (R03): REAL, S.O. 347(E), 26 Jan 2022. **"8–12 week certification" is NOT stated.** What the rules state:
  - Drone Rules rule 9(2): QCI/testing entity submits its test report "within sixty days from the date of receipt of the application".
  - Rule 9(3): DGCA issues the type certificate "within fifteen days of receiving such test report". That is a statutory maximum of ~75 days (~10.7 weeks).
  - CSUAS adds: deficiencies are flagged "within seven days of receipt of application"; applications are closed "if Initial Evaluation is not carried out within two months (60 days)"; and certification can be held for non-compliance "until a maximum period of sixty days".
  - Suggested wording: "statutory ceiling ~75 days (Drone Rules r.9)".
- **DGFT Notif. 54/2015-2020** (R11): REAL, 9 Feb 2022. "Import of drones in CBU, SKD or CKD form is Prohibited", with exceptions for R&D and defence & security. "Import of drone components shall be 'Free'".
- **Drone PLI** (R12): REAL, S.O. 4044(E), 30 Sep 2021.
  - "total financial outlay… INR 120 crore" over three FYs.
  - "PLI rate applicable to the eligible value addition… shall be 20%"; the minimum value addition is 40% of net sales.
  - The cap is Rs 30 Cr per manufacturer (25% of outlay).
  - Correction: "20%" is the incentive rate on value addition, not a 20% value-addition threshold.
